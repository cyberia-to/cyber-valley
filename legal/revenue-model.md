# Cyber Valley Revenue Model

> Status. Working model for discussion. Items rendered in italics were marked in the source as requiring separate discussion and approval. CEO remuneration was carried here in the source and now lives in [[ceo-compensation-schedule]].

Purpose of the document: to fix the principal revenue sources of PT Cyber Valley Estate and the holding, and the principles for distributing cash flow.

Part of the [[dzin]] corporate pack. The distribution waterfall in §2 sits under Articles 18 and 19 of the [[articles-of-association]]; the vocabulary in §5 governs every percentage in [[ceo-compensation-schedule]].

## 1. Core revenue model

### 1.1. Sale of land plots / hak sewa

Revenue is generated by the sale of individual land plots. See [[hak sewa]] and [[leasehold upfront]] for the product side.

Payment options:

- full payment;
- payment in parts / instalments under a separately approved financial model.

For instalments the following are to be defined separately:

- the down payment;
- the instalment term;
- interest / indexation;
- the payment schedule;
- the consequences of late payment and default;
- the procedure for terminating the transaction;
- the fate of payments already made;
- renewal / extension;
- transfer / assignment;
- the obligations of the parties for infrastructure and upkeep of the territory.

### 1.3. Short-term rental and events

Separate operational revenue may be generated by:

- short-term use of land (daily, weekly, monthly);
- holding events;
- rental of venues and infrastructure;
- other temporary forms of commercial use of the territory.

### 1.4. Infrastructure and utility fees

A separate revenue source is the provision of infrastructure and services to projects and residents of Cyber Valley, including:

- water;
- electricity;
- internet;
- roads;
- parking;
- waste management;
- maintenance and other common services.

Infrastructure charges are to be separated from business revenue share and, where possible, calculated on a comprehensible model: actual consumption, a fixed service fee, or an agreed tariff.

### 1.5. Revenue share from independent sub-projects

Businesses and projects operating on the territory of Cyber Valley may pay the holding a separate revenue share / community fee for access to the territory, the ecosystem, the brand, the client flow, the common infrastructure and other advantages of the project.

Possible sub-projects:

- SPA and wellness;
- restaurants and cafés;
- farms and plantations;
- production;
- coworking;
- glamping;
- event projects;
- construction and rental of villas;
- other entrepreneurial projects.

Revenue share is to use a pre-defined calculation base — for example Gross Revenue or Net Revenue — with permitted deductions clearly defined. Using "profit" as the base is advisable only where a transparent and controllable accounting policy exists.

### 1.6. Company-operated businesses

The holding or its subsidiaries may own and operate individual businesses on the territory of Cyber Valley directly. The income of such companies is accounted for as a separate source of the group's operating profit.

## 2. Distribution of cash and profit

Cash received by the company is to be held back from automatic distribution as dividends immediately on receipt.

Recommended order of distribution:

1. taxes and mandatory payments;
2. direct costs of the relevant transaction or project;
3. the approved operating budget;
4. development / infrastructure reserve;
5. the company's minimum cash reserve;
6. only then — distributable cash: dividends or other payments to shareholders.

*Proposed model for discussion: after covering mandatory costs, reserves and the approved budget, the remaining distributable cash is split in an agreed proportion between dividends to shareholders and reinvestment into the development of the project.*

## 4. Future and strategic revenue streams

### 4.1. Grants

Grants are to be accounted for separately from the company's commercial revenue. Where a grant is earmarked, the funds are used only within the relevant programme and stand outside the company's free distributable income.

*A separate decision is needed on whether grants may be received directly by the PT PMA, or whether a separate Yayasan / NGO structure is appropriate for part of the social and environmental projects.*

### 4.2. Carbon and environmental credits

A potential long-term revenue source from carbon credits, environmental credits, biodiversity and conservation projects, and other environmental instruments, where legally and technically realisable.

### 4.3. Tokens and digital assets

A potential long-term instrument for financing and monetising the ecosystem. Any model is to be developed separately, taking account of applicable law, taxation and digital asset regulation.

### 4.4. Franchise and licensing

In the medium term Cyber Valley may earn income from licensing the brand, know-how and operating model, and from developing a franchise model for other locations.

## 5. Principles for calculation

A single vocabulary is to be used for all percentage payments and revenue share across the agreements, including every commission in [[ceo-compensation-schedule]]. This section owns the definitions; other documents use them unchanged.

The following are to be defined in advance:

- Gross Revenue;
- Net Revenue;
- Profit;
- Transaction Value;
- Cash Received;
- Referral;
- Closing;
- Direct Costs;
- Taxes;
- Refunds and cancellations.

Where possible, commissions and revenue share are to be calculated from objectively verifiable figures rather than from figures one party can materially move through internal costs or accounting policy.

## 6. Questions for discussion and approval

Holding-level questions. Everything touching CEO remuneration moved to [[ceo-compensation-schedule]] §14.

*1. On income arriving in the company: 50% goes to dividend distribution, 50% into the project treasury for operations.*

*2. What revenue share independent Cyber Valley sub-projects pay: 5%, 10% — details to be written out.*

*3. What base the sub-project revenue share is calculated from: Gross Revenue, Net Revenue or another figure?*

*4. Whether charges for water, electricity and other infrastructure are to be levied separately from revenue share.*

*5. What minimum cash reserve the company must retain before distributing dividends.*

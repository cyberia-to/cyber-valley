# Cyber Valley Revenue Model

> Status. Working model for discussion. Section 6 lists what still needs approval; everything outside it is settled enough to build on.

Purpose of the document: to fix the principal revenue sources of PT Cyber Valley Estate and the holding, and the principles for distributing cash flow. CEO remuneration lives in [[ceo-compensation-schedule]].

Part of the [[dzin]] corporate pack. The distribution waterfall in §2 sits under Articles 18 and 19 of the [[articles-of-association]]; the vocabulary in §4 governs every percentage in [[ceo-compensation-schedule]].

## 1. Core revenue model

### 1.1. Sale of land plots and hak sewa

Revenue is generated by the sale of individual land plots. See [[hak sewa]] and [[leasehold upfront]] for the product side.

Payment runs either as a single payment in full, or in instalments under a separately approved financial model. The instalment model defines: the down payment; the term; interest and indexation; the payment schedule; the consequences of late payment and default; the procedure for terminating the transaction; the fate of payments already made; renewal and extension; transfer and assignment; and the obligations of the parties for infrastructure and upkeep of the territory.

### 1.2. Short-term rental and events

Separate operational revenue comes from short-term use of the land by the day, week or month; from holding events; from renting venues and infrastructure; and from other temporary commercial use of the territory.

### 1.3. Infrastructure and utility fees

The holding supplies infrastructure and services to projects and residents: water, electricity, internet, roads, parking, waste management, maintenance and other common services.

Infrastructure charges stay separate from business revenue share and are calculated on one comprehensible basis — actual consumption, a fixed service fee, or an agreed tariff.

### 1.4. Revenue share from independent sub-projects

Businesses operating on the territory pay the holding a revenue share or community fee for access to the territory, the ecosystem, the brand, the client flow and the common infrastructure. Sub-projects include SPA and wellness, restaurants and cafés, farms and plantations, production, coworking, glamping, event projects, and construction and rental of villas.

Revenue share uses a pre-defined calculation base from §4 — Gross Revenue or Net Revenue — with permitted deductions defined. Profit serves as a base only where a transparent and controllable accounting policy exists.

### 1.5. Company-operated businesses

The holding and its subsidiaries own and operate businesses on the territory directly. The income of those companies is accounted for as a separate source of the group's operating profit.

## 2. Distribution of cash and profit

Cash received by the company passes through the waterfall below before any of it becomes distributable:

1. taxes and mandatory payments;
2. direct costs of the relevant transaction or project;
3. the approved operating budget;
4. development and infrastructure reserve;
5. the company's minimum cash reserve;
6. distributable cash: dividends and other payments to shareholders.

The split of distributable cash between dividends and reinvestment is set by the General Meeting under [[articles-of-association]] Article 18, and the proportion is item 1 of §6.

## 3. Future and strategic revenue streams

### 3.1. Grants

Grants are accounted for separately from commercial revenue. An earmarked grant is spent only within its programme and stays outside distributable income. Whether grants are received by the PT PMA directly, or through a separate Yayasan or NGO structure for the social and environmental programmes, is item 6 of §6.

### 3.2. Carbon and environmental credits

A long-term revenue source from carbon credits, environmental credits, biodiversity and conservation projects, and other environmental instruments, available where legally and technically realisable.

### 3.3. Tokens and digital assets

A long-term instrument for financing and monetising the ecosystem. Any model is developed separately under applicable law, taxation and digital asset regulation.

### 3.4. Franchise and licensing

In the medium term Cyber Valley earns from licensing the brand, know-how and operating model, and from a franchise model for other locations.

## 4. Principles for calculation

One vocabulary governs every percentage payment and revenue share across the agreements, including every commission in [[ceo-compensation-schedule]]. This section owns the definitions; other documents use them unchanged.

The terms defined in advance are: Gross Revenue, Net Revenue, Profit, Transaction Value, Cash Received, Referral, Closing, Direct Costs, Taxes, and Refunds and cancellations.

Commissions and revenue share are calculated from objectively verifiable figures rather than from figures one party can materially move through internal costs or accounting policy.

## 5. Relation to CEO remuneration

The holding's revenue sources and the waterfall live here. The CEO's share of them — land commissions, sub-project referral and administration fees, the investment fee, salary, KPI bonuses, equity and exit economics — lives in [[ceo-compensation-schedule]], the single source of truth for every figure of CEO remuneration.

## 6. Open items

1. The split of distributable cash between dividends and the project treasury for operations; the working proposal is 50/50.
2. The revenue share paid by independent sub-projects, written out by category rather than as a single rate.
3. The base that sub-project revenue share is calculated from: Gross Revenue, Net Revenue or another figure from §4.
4. Whether charges for water, electricity and other infrastructure are levied separately from revenue share.
5. The minimum cash reserve the company retains before distributing dividends.
6. Whether grants are received by the PT PMA directly or through a separate Yayasan or NGO structure.
